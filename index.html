02 / Method

Seven sheets, seven ways of looking beneath paint

Each sheet stands for one stage in how a painting was traditionally built. Conservators study each stage with a different instrument. Since the 1920s Harvard's Fogg Museum has been one of the places where this science began: Alan Burroughs X-rayed paintings there, and Rutherford Gettens and George Stout worked on pigments, today's Straus Center for Conservation and Technical Studies.

    These sheets are computational analogies. Your browser generates them from the museum's photograph using edge detection, tonal posterisation and colour quantisation. They are not scans of the real paint layers. They show what each stage does to a picture, not what a particular painter actually laid down.

    03 / Light

    Why a lamp puts the picture back together

    Light through layers

    Oil paint is partly transparent. Light enters a glaze, passes through the colour beneath, bounces off a pale ground and comes back out filtered by every layer it crossed. That is why Van Eyck's or Titian's glazes seem to glow from within. In Lumen-Strata the lamp shines through all seven transparent sheets, and the image that lands on the wall is the painting. The whole is the sum of its strata.

    The camera obscura

    Pass light through a small opening into a dark room and an image of the outside world appears on the far wall, upside down. Mozi and Aristotle noted the effect. Ibn al-Haytham (c. 1011–1021) explained it, and Giambattista della Porta (1558) recommended it to painters. Whether Vermeer traced from one is still debated (Philip Steadman, Vermeer's Camera, 2001; the Hockney–Falco thesis). Switch the viewer's lens to Obscura to see the inversion.

    Seeing the invisible

    Technical art history also depends on light, just at wavelengths we can't see. X-rays (Röntgen, 1895) show dense lead white. Infrared passes through paint and is absorbed by carbon drawing. Ultraviolet makes aged varnish fluoresce. Every instrument in the Method list is a different kind of lamp.
